# 8. Motivation, comfort and inclusion

**Decide:** how do all learners stay in the lesson, and stay well?

## 8.1 Plan an early success

**Do**

- Make the first actions easy and clearly successful.
- Show progress, for example *"Step 3 of 8"*.

**In ALF:** in the panel text: [Write the instructions](../tools/scenario-setup/how-to/write-the-instructions.md). A sound or light when something works: [Play a sound or effect when a part moves](../tools/xr-kinematics/how-to/react-to-movement.md).

**Why:** experiencing success is the strongest source of confidence, and confidence predicts whether learners keep going. *Self-efficacy (Bandura), ARCS: confidence (Keller).*

## 8.2 Say why it matters

**Do:** in the first step, connect the task to the learners' lab, job or next course in one sentence.

**In ALF:** the first panel, marked **Initial**: [Write the instructions](../tools/scenario-setup/how-to/write-the-instructions.md).

**Why:** learners invest more when they see the relevance for themselves. *ARCS: relevance (Keller), Self-Determination Theory.*

## 8.3 Keep learners comfortable

**Do**

- Don't move the learner's view for them. Let them walk or turn themselves.
- Keep panels and controls within easy reach and at a comfortable height.
- Keep each headset session short, and plan breaks between stages.
- For learners who feel unwell easily, choose a player setup where they don't move through the virtual room.

**In ALF**

- Player setups with and without moving around the room: [Scene Generation: The five player setups](../tools/scene-generation/how-to/before-you-start.md#the-five-player-setups).
- Panel distance and placement: [Place the instruction panel](../tools/scenario-setup/how-to/place-the-instructions.md).

**Avoid:** long sessions with a lot of movement through the virtual room.

**Why:** motion sickness adds load and cuts sessions short. Comfort is a condition for learning, not a nicety. *Simulator sickness (Kennedy et al.; LaViola).*

## 8.4 Design for seated and one-hand use from the start

**Do**

- Place buttons, parts and panels so they can be reached while sitting.
- Don't require two hands unless using two hands is part of the goal.

**In ALF**

- Button position and height: [Move and rotate a button](../tools/button-injection/how-to/move-and-rotate.md).
- Panel position: [Place the instruction panel](../tools/scenario-setup/how-to/place-the-instructions.md).
- Two-handed resizing is optional per object: [Choose what learners can do with an object](../tools/object-spawner/how-to/choose-what-learners-can-do.md).

**Why:** designing for different bodies from the start is easier and better than adapting for individuals later. *Universal Design for Learning (CAST, UDL 3.0).*

## 8.5 Make language easy

**Do**

- Use the **same word for the same thing** everywhere: on panels, on labels, in the real stage.
- Label parts with their technical name, on the part.
- Support key steps with an image, not only words.
- Keep sentences short and built the same way.

**In ALF:** [Add a label](../tools/button-injection/how-to/add-a-label.md), [Write the instructions: Image](../tools/scenario-setup/how-to/write-the-instructions.md#image). For a second language, make a copy of the scene with translated panels, and add it to the [main menu](../tools/main-menu/how-to/create-the-main-menu.md).

**Why:** learners learn a subject through its vocabulary. Consistent words and several ways of presenting content let more learners in. *Language-sensitive teaching, UDL: multiple means of representation.*

## 8.6 Choose videos with care

**Do:** pick videos with captions, keep them short, and make sure the headset is online during the lesson.

**In ALF:** [Write the instructions: Video](../tools/scenario-setup/how-to/write-the-instructions.md#video) explains that videos play from YouTube.

**Why:** captions help learners with another first language and learners in noisy rooms. Short videos are easier to follow and to watch again. *UDL, segmenting (Mayer).*

## 8.7 Short on time? Cut, don't compress

**Do:** fewer steps, one task, no free exploration. A short lesson with a clear success is better than a long one done in a hurry.

**Why:** overloaded learners learn less, however immersive the scene. *Cognitive Load Theory, CAMIL (Makransky & Petersen).*
