# 1. Start from the goal

**Decide:** what should learners be able to do at the end, and how will you know they can?

An XR lesson is easy to fill with impressive content. A clear goal is what keeps it focused. Everything you build should serve it, and what doesn't serve it can go.

## 1.1 Write the goal as one observable action

**Do**

- Write one sentence that starts with *"Learners can…"*, followed by an action you can see: *assemble*, *set up*, *find*, *explain*, *choose*. Avoid verbs you can't observe, like *know* or *understand*.
- Add **where** and **how well**: on the real device or only in the headset; with help or alone; in any time or within a time limit.
- Keep the sentence where you see it while building. Check every step against it.

**In ALF:** the goal is the lesson's purpose, so give the lesson a name that says it. The scene's file name becomes the button text in the [main menu](../tools/main-menu/how-to/before-you-start.md).

**Avoid:** goals that describe the content (*"Learners learn about the machine"*) instead of what learners do.

**Why:** learning works best when the goal, the activities and the check all aim at the same action at the same level. *Constructive Alignment (Biggs).*

## 1.2 Decide how you'll check the goal, before you build

**Do**

- List what an expert would watch for when a learner does the task: the critical actions, their order, and the safety points.
- Use this list as the **final check** of the lesson, ideally on the real object.
- Mark the items on the list where beginners usually go wrong. Those steps get the most support later.

**In ALF:** the checklist is used outside ALF, in the last stage. Inside ALF, it tells you which steps need the most help (see [Plan the support](./plan-the-support.md)) and where to warn about mistakes (see [Plan for mistakes](./plan-for-mistakes.md)).

**Avoid:** checking something else than you practised, for example a written test for a hands-on skill.

**Why:** planning backwards from the evidence keeps the whole lesson on target. *Backward design (Wiggins & McTighe), Constructive Alignment.*

## 1.3 Know what kind of goal it is

Different goals need different lessons. Find the row that fits your goal best:

| Learners should… | Example, in general terms | The lesson should mainly… |
|---|---|---|
| **Carry out a fixed procedure** | Same steps, same order, every time | Show it done, then let learners practise with less and less help, until they do it fluently. |
| **Solve a task with decisions** | Find a fault, choose a method, adapt to the situation | Offer varied cases, ask *"what would you do?"*, and show how an expert reasons. |
| **Understand how something works** | Explain, predict or reason about a system | Let learners predict, try and observe, then explain the difference. |
| **Recognise and tell apart** | Name parts, spot defects, distinguish states | Compare similar cases side by side, and let learners choose from look-alikes. |
| **Follow rules and act safely** | Behave correctly in a risky or critical situation | Give the reason behind every rule and show the consequences safely. |

Many lessons combine two, for example *understand* first and then *carry out*. Plan the understanding part first.

**In ALF:** [Let learners act](./let-learners-act.md) lists the tools for each kind of goal. [Which tool for which teaching goal](./which-tool.md) gives a quick overview.

**Why:** each type of learning outcome needs its own teaching strategy. *Component Display Theory (Merrill), 4C/ID (van Merriënboer & Kirschner), revised Bloom taxonomy.*

## 1.4 Show the whole before the parts

**Do**

- Begin the lesson with an overview: what learners will do, why it matters, and what the finished result looks like.
- Show the whole system or the whole task once, before going into details.

**In ALF**

- An [exploded view that opens at the first step](../tools/exploded-view/how-to/open-at-a-lesson-step.md) shows how a machine is built.
- A short video on the first instruction panel: [Write the instructions: Video](../tools/scenario-setup/how-to/write-the-instructions.md#video).
- A [video screen](../tools/youtube-player/how-to/add-a-video-screen.md) by the entrance, for an introduction that stays in the room.

**The learner experiences:** they know where they're going before the first instruction, and can place each step in the bigger picture.

**Avoid:** starting straight with step 1 of the procedure.

**Why:** new knowledge sticks better when it can be hung onto an overview given beforehand. *Advance organiser (Ausubel), Gagné's nine events (gain attention, state the objective, recall prior knowledge).*
