# The theory behind the guidelines

The [guidelines](./overview.md) come from learning research. This page shows which models stand behind them, in plain words. You don't need to read it to use the guidelines.

## The big idea: the same task, with less and less help

ALF lessons follow one idea, called **Fade to Real**: learners do the **same task** several times, and each time

- the **help goes down** (fewer instructions, shorter hints),
- the **realism goes up** (from a virtual room, to digital help on the real object, to the real object alone),
- the **learner takes over** more decisions.

| Stage | Where | Help | What it's for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Orient** | Before or at the start | Full | See the whole task, the goal and the background. |
| **Practise in VR** | Virtual room | High, then less | Learn the steps safely; mistakes cost nothing. |
| **Transfer in AR** | Real room, digital help on the real object | Low | Do the task on the real object, with labels where they're needed. |
| **Perform for real** | Real object, no headset | None | Show you can do it. This is the check of the goal. |

Not every lesson needs every stage. See [Choose the stages](./choose-the-stages.md).

Three models carry this idea:

- **4C/ID** (van Merriënboer & Kirschner): complex skills are learned through whole tasks. Help decreases within a series of tasks, and realism increases across them. Background knowledge is given beforehand; step instructions just in time.
- **Cognitive Apprenticeship** (Collins, Brown & Newman): an expert shows, coaches and supports, then steps back. Learners put their thinking into words and reflect.
- **Constructive Alignment** (Biggs): the goal, what learners do, and how you check it must all aim at the same action.

## What each part of the guidelines is based on

| Guideline | Model | In one sentence | What it means for your lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| Goal | Constructive Alignment | Goal, activity and check aim at the same action. | One goal sentence; the final checklist checks it. |
| Goal | Revised Bloom; psychomotor levels | Goals differ in what they ask: remember, understand, apply… or imitate, refine, master. | Procedures follow the fading path; understanding follows the inquiry path. |
| Orientation | Advance organiser (Ausubel); Gagné's nine events | New knowledge anchors to an overview given first. | Start with the whole task or system. |
| Stages | 4C/ID; simulation fidelity | Whole tasks, support fading, fidelity rising. Doing the right actions matters more than realistic looks. | Same task in every stage; make actions and consequences faithful. |
| Stages | Transfer: identical elements, hugging and bridging (Thorndike; Perkins & Salomon) | Skills carry over when practice resembles the target, and further when learners reflect on what generalises. | Same order and words in every stage; end with reflection. |
| Each step | Cognitive Load Theory (Sweller) | Working memory is small. Bad design wastes it. | One action per step; show only what's needed. |
| Each step | Multimedia principles (Mayer) | Segmenting, signaling, spatial contiguity, coherence, redundancy, pre-training. | Short steps, instructions next to the part, no duplicated content, practise the controls first. |
| Each step | CAMIL (Makransky & Petersen) | Immersion raises interest and presence, but can also overload. | Immersion helps only if the design is lean. |
| Let learners act | ICAP (Chi & Wylie) | Interactive > constructive > active > passive. | Finish steps with an action; add predictions and discussions. |
| Let learners act | Embodied cognition (Johnson-Glenberg) | Gestures that match the real action support learning. | Hands for hand tasks. |
| Let learners act | Predict–Observe–Explain; guided inquiry | Predicting exposes misconceptions; exploration needs guidance. | Ask before you show; explore with a goal. |
| Let learners act | Multiple external representations (Ainsworth) | Graphs and 3D scenes help together only when learners are shown how they relate. | Point out what in the scene matches what in the graph. |
| Support and fading | Modeling (Bandura); Cognitive Apprenticeship | People learn by watching a model, when attention is guided and they can replay it. | Record a demonstration with your explanation; let learners pause and replay. |
| Support and fading | Worked examples and backward fading (Renkl & Atkinson) | Beginners learn from examples; remove help from the end first. | Show first, then fade the last steps first. |
| Support and fading | Expertise reversal (Kalyuga) | Help for beginners hinders advanced learners. | Offer different levels of help. |
| Mistakes | Feedback research (Hattie & Timperley; Shute) | Feedback works when it's specific, about the task, and timely. | Name typical errors at the step; debrief. |
| Mistakes | Contrasting cases (Schwartz & Bransford) | Comparing near-identical cases reveals the critical feature. | Show right and wrong side by side. |
| Close the loop | Kolb's learning cycle; retrieval practice | Experience needs reflection; recalling strengthens memory. | Reflection step; recall at the next session. |
| Motivation | Self-Determination Theory; ARCS; self-efficacy | Autonomy, competence and relatedness; early success builds confidence. | Early success, relevance, pair work. |
| Inclusion | UDL 3.0; language-sensitive teaching | Plan for different learners from the start. | Seated reach, consistent terms, pictures as well as words. |
| Comfort | Simulator sickness (Kennedy et al.) | Motion sickness adds load and shortens sessions. | Short sessions, no forced camera movement. |

## What ALF can't do yet

The guidelines mark recommendations that touch on missing features with **Not built in yet**, and give a workaround:

- **No reaction to wrong actions.** A wrong press does nothing. Workaround: name typical mistakes on the step, and debrief.
- **No automatic fading.** Help doesn't adapt to how a learner performs. Workaround: separate scenes per run or level, ordered in the main menu, and learner choice.
- **No answers in the headset.** Learners can't type or record a prediction or reflection. Workaround: aloud, in pairs or on paper.
