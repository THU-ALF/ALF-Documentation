# 7. Close the loop

**Decide:** how does the lesson end, and how does it carry over to the real task?

Most XR lessons stop when the last step is done. Learning is consolidated afterwards: when learners look back, compare, and use it again.

## 7.1 End with a reflection step

**Do**

- Make the last step a reflection, with two questions, for example:
  - *"What was the hardest step, and why?"*
  - *"What will you do differently on the real one?"*
- Learners answer to a partner or the group, after taking the headset off.

**In ALF:** the last step (marked **End**) with a text panel, placed in front of the learner with **Player Attached**: [Place the instruction panel](../tools/scenario-setup/how-to/place-the-instructions.md).

**The learner experiences:** a moment to sort what they did, before the next thing starts.

**Avoid:** ending with *"Well done!"* and nothing else.

**Why:** without reflection, learners mainly manage the exact task they practised. Reflection builds the bridge to new situations. *Bridging (Perkins & Salomon), Kolb's learning cycle, Cognitive Apprenticeship: reflection.*

:::note[Not built in yet]
Learners can't type answers in the headset. Do the reflection aloud, in pairs, or on paper.
:::

## 7.2 Finish on the real task

**Do**

- If the real object is available, the last stage is the real task: same order, same words as in the headset.
- Use the checklist from [1.2](./start-from-the-goal.md#12-decide-how-youll-check-the-goal-before-you-build), by a supervisor, a partner or the learner.

**In ALF:** outside ALF. In AR, the step in between: digital help lying on the real object, made more see-through each run: [Positioning Setup](../tools/positioning/how-to/before-you-start.md), [Set transparency](../tools/positioning/how-to/appearance-and-movement.md#make-the-model-see-through).

**Avoid:** a final check that is different from what was practised.

**Why:** skills carry over best when practice and the real task share as many elements as possible. *Identical elements (Thorndike), hugging (Perkins & Salomon), Constructive Alignment.*

## 7.3 Bring learners back to recall

**Do**

- If learners come back for another session, start with a few steps **without help**: *"What comes next?"*
- Only then continue with the guided part.

**In ALF:** a short scene, or the first steps of a scenario with only cues on the panels: [Write the instructions](../tools/scenario-setup/how-to/write-the-instructions.md).

**Why:** recalling after a gap strengthens memory more than going through the material again. *Retrieval practice (Roediger & Karpicke), spacing effect.*

## 7.4 Pair learners for the debrief

**Do**

- Let learners work in pairs: one does the task, the other watches with the checklist. Then they switch.
- End with a short comparison: what did each notice?

**In ALF:** outside ALF. The checklist is the one from [1.2](./start-from-the-goal.md#12-decide-how-youll-check-the-goal-before-you-build).

**Why:** explaining and discussing is the most effective engagement mode, and motivation grows when learners feel connected, not just competent. *ICAP: interactive (Chi & Wylie), Self-Determination Theory (Ryan & Deci).*
